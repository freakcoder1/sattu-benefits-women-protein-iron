## Body

### What Is Sattu?
Sattu is a flour made from roasted black chickpeas (kala chana). It is a traditional food in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, and West Bengal, where it is made into sattu sharbat (a cooling drink), litti, and parathas.

### Nutritional Profile (per 100g)
- Protein: 20-25g (chickpea-based)
- Carbohydrates: 60-65g (mostly complex)
- Fiber: 5-8g
- Iron: 4-5mg
- Magnesium: 120-140mg
- Calcium: 50-60mg
- Low glycemic index: 35-45

### Benefits for Women
**Plant-based protein:** At 20-25g protein per 100g, sattu is comparable to urad dal and significantly higher than regular atta. A two-tablespoon serving provides about 6-7g of protein - more than a glass of milk.

**Iron support:** Sattu contains 4-5mg of iron per 100g. For menstruating women, where iron deficiency anaemia is common, sattu can be a useful dietary addition. Pairing it with lemon juice (vitamin C) improves iron absorption by up to 3-6 times.

**Digestive regularity:** The fiber in sattu supports gut health and helps prevent constipation, which is a common concern for women taking iron supplements.

**Satiety and weight management:** The combination of protein and fiber promotes fullness, making sattu useful for managing appetite.

### How to Use Sattu
- **Sattu sharbat:** Mix 2-3 tablespoons of sattu in a glass of water or buttermilk. Add lemon, black salt, roasted cumin powder, and mint.
- **Sattu with milk:** Mix like protein powder for a nutritious drink.
- **In rotis or litti:** Mix sattu flour with water to make a filling for whole wheat rotis.

### Evidence Quality
Most research supporting sattu benefits comes from studies on chickpeas, dietary fiber, and plant protein - not clinical trials using sattu itself. A 2012 review in the British Journal of Nutrition (Jukanti et al.) found chickpea protein quality rates above other pulses. A 2014 review by Mudryj et al. confirmed pulses provide protein, fiber, iron, zinc, folate, and magnesium. The ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) count fiber-rich foods like pulses among the things that improve satiety. Sattu should be viewed as a whole-food nutrition option, not a treatment for anaemia or any medical condition.

### Who Should Avoid Sattu
People with chickpea allergies, diagnosed kidney disease requiring protein restriction, or IBS triggered by high-FODMAP foods should consult a doctor.
